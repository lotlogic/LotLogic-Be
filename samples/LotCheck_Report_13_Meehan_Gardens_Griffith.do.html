ZONING

Your block is zoned RZ1 – Suburban Zone.

RZ1 is the most common residential zone in Canberra, covering established suburban areas. Historically, RZ1 has been restricted to one main dwelling per block, with limited options for adding a second home. The ACT Government's Missing Middle reforms have changed this — removing minimum block size requirements and opening up new possibilities for dual occupancy, secondary dwellings, and in some cases, unit titling.

Understanding your zone is the foundation for knowing what's possible. The sections below assess your specific block against both current rules and the new reforms.

TREES

Large trees visible: Several    |    Location: Multiple locations

Based on the latest aerial imagery, there appear to be several significant trees on your block, located in multiple locations across the site.

Trees in the ACT are protected under the Urban Forest Act 2023 if they meet any of the following criteria:

• 8 metres or taller

• Canopy width of 8 metres or more

• Trunk circumference of 1 metre or more (measured at 1.4m above ground)

Protected trees have a tree protection zone that includes the area under the canopy plus 4 metres from the trunk in all directions. You cannot build, excavate, or disturb roots within this zone without approval from the ACT Tree Protection Unit.

If any of your trees are protected, any new dwelling or driveway would need to be positioned to avoid the protection zone. The presence of multiple trees may further constrain layout options. We can help you confirm tree status and assess how they affect your options as part of a detailed feasibility review.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOUR BLOCK

Based on the assessment above, here is what you could realistically achieve:

✘ Add a granny flat behind the existing house: Unlikely. The existing house (457 m²) leaves only 17.5 m² of remaining site coverage — well below the minimum needed for a granny flat. The minimal rear yard depth (5.2m) and multiple trees further constrain this option.

✘ Dual occupancy (keeping existing house): Unlikely. Site coverage is exhausted, rear yard space is minimal, and tree locations may restrict building envelopes.

✔ Dual occupancy (removing existing house): Possible. Your block at 949 m² with 19m frontage has the headline metrics for dual occupancy under the reforms. Removing the existing house would reset site coverage to the full 50% (474.5 m²), providing ample room for two dwellings. Tree locations would need to be worked around in the design.

✔ Unit titling (two separate titles): Possible. At 949 m², your block exceeds the 600m² threshold for unit titling under the reforms. This would allow you to build two dwellings and sell them separately — subject to design, access, and approval.

✔ Three dwellings: Possible in theory. At 949 m², your block exceeds the 800m² threshold. However, fitting three dwellings while managing tree protection zones, rear sewer access, and parking would be challenging. A detailed feasibility review would confirm whether this is realistic.

Summary: Your block has strong underlying metrics for redevelopment under the Missing Middle reforms. The key constraint is the existing house — its size and position leave almost no room for additions while it remains. The most viable pathway is a knockdown-rebuild scenario, designing around the trees and rear sewer location.
